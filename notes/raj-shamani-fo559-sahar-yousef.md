# Neuroscientist's Guide To 10X Your Focus & Memory

**Guest:** Dr. Sahar Yousef (Cognitive Neuroscientist, UC Berkeley) · **Host:** Raj Shamani · Figuring Out FO559 · 8 Sep 2026 · 2h 55m
**Watch:** [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Vz6L8B73i4) · **Chronotype test:** [mychronotype.com](https://www.mychronotype.com) (code FIGURINGOUT)

> **Core idea:** Your brain runs on biological laws, like gravity. Successful people learn and follow those laws instead of fighting them.

## 1. The "Billionaire Brain" = Belief
- #1 difference: **belief**. Ultra-achievers see no limits — "where others see a brick wall, they see a door."
- **Your brain looks for data that confirms your beliefs** (works for negative ones too).
- **Redirection:** when the victim story starts ("I come from nothing, I'll fail") → tell your brain "zip it" and redirect attention to the story you want.
- **Write your new story** from the view of the successful version of you — even if you don't believe it yet. Review it daily.
- Science: the **DMN (default mode network)** is the brain's storyteller. Repeating your story *primes* the brain to notice opportunities that feel "random".

## 2. Build a New Habit — 4 Steps (sleep example)
1. **Identity:** "I prioritise my health because it drives my performance."
2. **Data:** what you know is true ("sleep makes me sharper").
3. **Benefit:** what gets better ("more focused, better questions").
4. **New habit as identity:** "I'm the type of person who sleeps 6–7 hours because it gives me an edge."

## 3. If-Then Plans Beat Vague Goals
- "I'll try to sleep earlier" = a prayer, not a plan.
- **Implementation intentions** (Peter Gollwitzer): **"WHEN X happens, THEN I do Y."**
- Describe it like a movie scene — time, place, what you're doing.
- **Make it tiny.** Example: brush teeth at 11 pm → kitchen closed → one step closer to bed.

## 4. Breaking Bad Habits
- Habit = addiction = a behaviour done many times. Same thing.
- Loop: **Feeling → Craving → Behaviour**. The craving isn't for Reels; it's to escape a feeling (overwhelm, stress, tiredness).
- Scrolling adds **shame** on top of overwhelm → loop again. Every repeat makes the neural path thicker, "like a tree trunk".
- **Breaking is harder than building** — you must weaken the old path AND build a new one.
- **How:**
  1. **Awareness** — a *cognitive pause*: "What am I feeling right now?"
  2. **Replacement** — 60 seconds of something else first (close eyes, breathe). You can still scroll after.
  3. Over months the old path gets thinner. This is **emotional regulation**.

## 5. Breaks
- Nature 2024 study: **scrolling during a break ≈ no break at all**. A walk / looking at nature actually restores.
- Tired at night → sleep. Tired midday → real rest, not the phone.

## 6. Your Phone Makes You Dumber — Even Switched Off
- **UT Austin study:** phone fully OFF — in another room vs. pocket vs. face-down on desk.
- **Visible on the desk** → attention **and general fluid intelligence** dropped significantly.
- Phones on the meeting table **lower the whole team's IQ**. People with phones out seem **less trustworthy, capable, intelligent**.
- **Rules:**
  - A "parking lot" / box for the phone during deep work.
  - **Phone OFF 15–30 min before bed**, and don't check it **15–30 min after waking**.
  - Morning is when your brain is most open to priorities and ideas — don't hand it to notifications.
  - Also kill laptop notifications; close Slack/email during focus.

## 7. Multitasking Is a Myth
- **Test:** write "I am a great multitasker" + numbers 1–20 → **17.7 s**. Alternate letter/number → **41.4 s** (more than double) + many mistakes.
- You have one attention system. It's really **task-switching** — every switch costs **time + energy**.
- **Stanford study:** people who said "I'm a great multitasker" **performed worse**.
- Many people who think they have ADHD may just have scroll-damaged attention (her view, not a diagnosis).

## 8. Ideas Come From Boredom
- Ultra-successful people don't have better ideas — they have **more**, because they protect attention. Ideas are a numbers game.
- **DMN** = mind-wandering / idea machine. **CEN (central executive network)** = the "CEO" that focuses and blocks distractions. They're opposites.
- **Schedule think time** (like Bill Gates' "think week"). A little boredom in the morning and before bed.
- **Shower-thought method:** load up everything about the problem → walk away → change a variable (water, walk, shower, cook) → no phone. Rewatching an old sitcom is fine; scrolling is not.
- **Content diet:** the algorithm shows you more of the same. Change your feed — "**bloom scrolling**" instead of doom scrolling.

## 9. Long-Form vs Short-Form
| Long-form (movies, podcasts) | Short-form (Reels/Shorts) |
|---|---|
| Story & narrative | Pure novelty |
| Memory → learning | No memory → no learning ("recall 3 Reels ago?") |
| Builds empathy | More anxiety & depressive symptoms |
| More connection | More loneliness |

- **Autobiographical planning** (thinking about your future story) happens when bored — kids scrolling rarely do it.
- **Why scrolling = lonely** (her hypothesis): craving connection → dopamine up → scrolling gives no **oxytocin** (bonding) or **serotonin** (contentment) → like eating candy when hungry; you end up hungrier. Real connection is the "real food".

## 10. Brain as a City
- **DMN** = story studio (meaning). **CEN** = city hall (CEO, goals).
- **Dorsal attention network** = watchtower (the spotlight of focus).
- **Salience network** = dispatch centre — notifications pull the spotlight away.
- **Amygdala** = alarm tower (fear). **Hippocampus** = library (memory). They sit close → emotional events are remembered better.
- **Better memory = sharper attention.** You only remember what you paid attention to.
- You can't switch these off — **control your environment**.

## 11. The "5 AM Club" Is a Myth for Most
- **Foundation = 7+ hours of quality sleep.** Wake time matters less. Contiguous sleep is best; before midnight gives extra benefit.
- 2017 Nobel Prize (Medicine): humans don't share one circadian rhythm → **chronotypes**.

| Type | Share | Pattern | Use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| AM-shifted | ~25% | Peak early, declines through the day | Hard work early |
| **Biphasic** | **Majority** | Mid-morning peak → strong afternoon dip → second evening peak | Deep work mid-morning; rest/exercise in the dip |
| PM-shifted | 10–15% | Slow mornings, evening peak | Creative/reading in morning; deep work in evening |

- **Peak window is only 2–3 hours a day — protect it.**
- **In the dip:** admin, calls, messages, errands. **Working out in the dip** makes the next peak stronger.

## 12. Stress vs Burnout
- Stress isn't bad — you need cortisol (without it you'd be in a coma).
- **"Stress doesn't kill. Never completing the stress cycle kills."** Stress should go up, then come down.
- Modern day (phone at wake-up → meetings → emails) never lets it come down.

## 13. The 3M Framework (prevent burnout)
| Break | How often | How long |
|---|---|---|
| **Micro** | Daily | ≥ 10 min |
| **Meso** | Weekly | 2–4 hours (2 is fine) |
| **Macro** | Monthly | Half day – full day |

- Only counts with **complete psychological detachment** — mentally "off the grid".
- **How:** do something **novel and challenging** — new run route, new recipe, learn an instrument. Same routine = mind drifts back to worries.
- Flow can also count as a break.

## 14. Her 9-Week Study (450 Berkeley students, unpublished)
- Limited short-form scrolling (friend gets a text if you exceed), 90-min movie with no second screen as homework, therapist-taught emotional regulation, recovery stories, in-person connection (party, hikes, blind dates), a new hobby.
- **Results:** **significantly less lonely, depressed, anxious**; **attention span and working memory up** — even though the retest was during midterms.
- Attention was measured with digital tests, not surveys → hard to fake.
- Her own limit: ~**10 min scrolling/day** + 20–30 min good long-form.

- Cost of ambition: friendships, travel, fun. Now prioritises her people.
